An in-silico story in six acts

Can pausing treatment
beat never stopping?

EvoDose is an AI experiment on prostate-cancer hormone therapy: a two-population tumor model fitted to real-style PSA histories, and a Meta Muse Agent that designs pause/restart dosing rules to outlast the standard schedule.

Scroll to begin ↓

Research tool only — not medical advice. Everything here is simulated. Nothing on this page is a treatment recommendation — do not change any therapy because of it.

Act I · The dilemma

Hormone therapy works — until it doesn't.

Continuous hormone therapy suppresses the tumor, but the cancer adapts and resistance grows. Intermittent therapy inserts drug holidays that give patients a break — yet the standard rule for when to pause and restart (stop at 50% below baseline, restart at baseline) is a fixed recipe, the same for every patient.

Why it matters: pausing at the wrong moment wastes the holiday; pausing at the right one may keep the tumor controlled longer while cutting total drug exposure.

Act II · The hidden enemy

Two cell populations, one blood test.

The model tracks drug-sensitive cells (S), which the drug kills, and resistant cells (R), which it can't. They compete for room to grow — and the PSA reading is just their sum, S + R. No blood test can see R directly. The fit uses an ensemble of 200 parameter sets, and the widest uncertainty is exactly where you'd expect: the hidden initial resistant fraction.

Why it matters: every dosing decision is made half-blind. The model infers the invisible resistant population from the PSA curve plus the on/off schedule — and carries its uncertainty along.

Act III · The twist

Why stopping can beat never stopping.

Killing every sensitive cell sounds thorough — but it clears the field for the resistant cells, a phenomenon called competitive release. A well-timed holiday keeps enough sensitive cells alive to crowd out the resistant ones, so the tumor stays controllable for longer.

Why it matters: this is the whole bet behind adaptive therapy — less drug, longer control — but only if the pauses are timed to the tumor's dynamics, not to a fixed recipe.

Scene 03 · The Meta Muse Agent

An AI proposes dosing rules. Only improvements survive.

Up to 10 iterations × 3 candidates per patient. Each rule is scored on 200 simulated tumor variants — median time to progression, worst-decile time to progression (TTP), and % time on drug. Objective: median TTP + 0.5 × worst-decile TTP − 0.2 × % weeks on drug.

    Coda · How it was made
    
    
    Methods in brief.
    
    
      
      
    • Model: weekly Lotka–Volterra competition. Sensitive cells S and resistant cells R;
        drug kills S only; PSA = S + R. Six fitted parameters per patient.
      • 
      
    • Fit on the first 60% of each patient's PSA history (differential evolution),
        then 200 Metropolis samples for uncertainty — widest on the hidden initial
        resistant fraction and the two competition strengths.
      • 
      
    • Validation predicts the held-out 40% with the real on/off schedule; reported
        as log-RMSE (prediction error on the log scale) and MAE
        (mean absolute error, in ng/mL) per patient and overall.
      • 
      
    • Baselines: continuous treatment, and stop-at-50% / restart-at-baseline.
      • 
      
    • Meta Muse Agent: Muse Spark via the Meta Model API when META_API_KEY is set,
        otherwise a built-in evolutionary search over new rule structures
        (slope triggers, minimum dwell times, hysteresis, holiday schedules).
      • 
      
    • Data shown is a synthetic Bruchovsky-style surrogate (same columns as the trial
        extract). Drop in the real CSV at data/bruchovsky_psa.csv and re-run
        python3 src/run_pipeline.py — see README.
